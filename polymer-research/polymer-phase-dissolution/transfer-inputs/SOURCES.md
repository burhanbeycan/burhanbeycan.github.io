# Dilute-solution data for polystyrene in cyclohexane

The CSV contains the five measurements of the B preparation in Table III (p. 3501) of McIntyre, O’Mara and Konouck, J. Am. Chem. Soc. 1959, 81, 3498–3503. The publication's tabulated quantity is 10⁴A₂, with A₂ in mol cm³ g⁻². Oscillations in the molar mass listed for the same fraction at different temperatures are experimental osmometric intercept estimates. These are number-average values and do not denote independently synthesized samples or nominal weight-average molar masses. The source gives no per-observation A₂ standard errors, osmometer pressure setpoint or fraction dispersity in Table III. Zero is a reported value at the table’s resolution. The membranes were gel cellophane and pressures were measured at equilibrium. The B fractions came from the same conventional bulk radical preparation (benzoyl peroxide). The A series prepared with mercaptan and the alternate thermally initiated C series are outside the selected B-series preparation. The 35 and 40 °C points are correlated within source; there are five values, three fractions, two temperature levels, and one laboratory. There is no claim of five independent studies.

The separate θ CSV is a literature check. Nakamura, Norisuye and Teramoto, Macromolecules 1991, 24, 4904–4908 studied six narrow PS fractions by light scattering between 27 and 45 °C. Their high-mass linear fractions (M_w roughly ≥50 kg/mol) yielded the same reported zero-crossing temperature within the authors’ ±0.3 °C tolerance. Treat this as one publication-level check. The reported tolerance is not assumed to be one Gaussian standard deviation. The molecular weights in cyclohexane for these fractions in Table I (p. 4906) range from about 50,700 to 3,000,000 g/mol. The low-mass fraction did not yield the same zero crossing and must not be used as support for this selected high-mass aggregate. Source light-scattering θ is independent of the membrane-osmometry and PS cloud-point sources.

For f = φ lnφ/N + (1−φ)ln(1−φ) + (A+B/T+Cφ)φ(1−φ), dimensionless osmotic pressure is φf′−f. Express φ as v̄_PS c with c in g/cm³, use solvent lattice molar volume V_s in cm³/mol. Then Π/(RT) = c/M_n + A₂c² + …, giving A₂ = (v̄_PS²/V_s)(½−A−B/T+C). This is the dilute osmotic coefficient. A measured binodal endpoint, a finite-concentration integral χ and the zero of A₂ are separate observables. If a temperature-independent solvent reference volume is employed it is a reported model convention.

Striolo, Prausnitz & Bertucco, Macromolecules 2000, 33, 9583–9586, provide an external membrane-osmometry series from a separate laboratory. The CSV copies the three LPSLM cyclohexane rows from Table 3 of the LBNL preprint (PDF page 27). At each temperature the intercept-fitted number-average molar mass and reported ± values are retained. Table 1 names the supplier’s SEC result “total molecular weight” (73,000 g/mol, dispersity 1.04) without specifying Mw or Mn in the header or footnote. Preserve that label. The three entries are one sample measured at three temperatures. Their B₂₂ was reported in 10⁻⁷ L mol g⁻²; multiply the published values by 10⁻⁴ for cm³ mol g⁻². The error type of the individual ± values is unspecified in the table. The apparatus temperature stability (±0.2 °C) is a separate instrument descriptor. Exclude methylcyclohexane, toluene and 8-arm branched polymer rows. A₂ is extracted by fitting dilute membrane osmotic pressure, not from their separate molecular simulation.

The finite-concentration pressure challenge uses two complete temperature series for fraction II (M_n = 72 kg/mol) of Krigbaum & Geymer (JACS 1959, 81, 1859–1868), Table I p. 1862. The authors specify the working temperatures for fraction II as 24, 34 and 44 °C. The selected 24 and 34 °C series were recoverable without disagreement in concentration and reduced pressure between the primary table extraction and its independent typesetting in Hiemenz & Rajagopalan, *Principles of Colloid and Surface Chemistry*, problem quoting Krigbaum & Geymer. The source's Fig. 2 axis and the textbook establish the scale Q = 10⁶ Π/(RTc) in mol/g. The web transcription of the original printed table misrecognizes some temperature digits and the power-of-ten scale. Numerical c and Q values are included only for the two complete comparable series. Ambiguous 44 °C series, ambiguous other columns and other fractions were not included. The authors identify membrane leakage for the lower-mass fraction I. Concentration c is the mass of polymer per mL of solution. Pressure is reconstructed from the published rounded c and Q pairs, Π(kPa) = 10⁻³ R T c Q, with R in J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ and T in K. A study-wide estimated 3% error is reported in the paper; per-observation experimental σ values are unavailable. Pressure is a separate response across a wide concentration range. If its residuals are used to select a model or tune a weight, the dataset ceases to be external. For direct comparison use the sample-specific Mn and the same volume convention and equilibrium / homogeneous branch convention as the model, and disclose ranges outside the cloud calibration.
