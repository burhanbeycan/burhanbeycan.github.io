# Sequence and hydration features in PVA film adsorption

**Research question.** Do sequence and polymer–water contacts help predict equilibrium adsorption in a grade that was entirely absent during calibration? The response is the mass of water per mass of dry partially acetylated PVA film, after equilibration at a declared water activity and temperature. Predictions apply to the declared adsorption branch of unfilled, comparably processed films. Use compatible molecular-mass and crystallinity methods across all records. The benchmark does not compute diffusion kinetics or evaporative drying.

## What the experimental sources support

| Record | Direct evidence | Role in this comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Nakajima and Gocho, Figs. 5–6 | Water-vapour isotherms after homogeneous and heterogeneous acetylation. At low RH the two families show similar capacity; at higher RH homogeneous samples take up more water. Their crystalline organization also varies. | Independent directional comparison. Preparation route cannot numerically substitute for NMR dyads. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1246/nikkashi.1978.1431) |
| Highly blocky AcPVOH series, 2026 | Batches with comparable acetylation levels and substantially different sequence statistics measured using NMR. | Supports a structural control. The extracted records do not supply same-specimen adsorption curves and atomistic coordination. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsapm.6c00317) |
| Chiessi, Cavalieri and Paradossi, 2007 | Atomistic simulations at 323 K of a highly hydrated crosslinked PVA hydrogel, with QENS comparison. | Hydrogel topology and water loading differ from those of the acetylated films. No descriptor is transferred. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1021/jp0671143) |

The verified intersection of these three sources contains zero complete specimen records. The GUI consequently gives no experimental three-model score. The adjacent synthetic benchmark tests the calculations while preserving this distinction.

## How to enter a study

Use the [specimen CSV](../src/polymer_transport_lab/data/pva_grade_spec.csv) and [observation CSV](../src/polymer_transport_lab/data/pva_grade_obs.csv) as templates. Every row of the supplied files is synthetic. Replace the contents of both boxes and select **User-supplied records** to analyze your own data.

1. Give each physical specimen an ID and, independently, a grade label that stays the same across studies using that grade. Supply OH repeat fraction, **number-average** molar mass (g/mol), and independently measured dry crystallinity (fraction). Supply only linear, partially acetylated films, with comparable film preparation, thermal history, crystallinity method and adsorption protocol. Use a separate cohort when those conditions change systematically.
2. Supply the quantitative NMR fraction of **mixed OH–OAc dyads in both orientations combined** and its original source for the same chemistry and batch. In a stationary chain with OH fraction h the upper limit is 2 min(h,1−h). The bound assumes negligible chain-end imbalance. No sequence is assigned from a manufacturer’s hydrolysis grade. Use a blank for missing features.
3. Give the mean number of water oxygen sites in the shell around one OH oxygen, then around one acetate **carbonyl** oxygen. Calculate for the same grade at one independently prespecified MD reference water loading (g/g dry polymer), temperature and protocol across all grades. Set md_origin to independent_reference, include simulation source and record force field, water model, radial cutoff and replica treatment in md_protocol or the cited record. Using the measured target uptake to set the simulated water content leaks the answer.
4. Enter equilibrated water mass per dry polymer mass (g/g) for every interior vapour activity and measurement temperature in kelvin. Keep original measurements with their producing study ID/source. Average technical replicates at identical conditions. Keep independently prepared films as separate specimens. Liquid contact, desorption, nonequilibrium gravimetry and film drying need their own experiments.
5. Choose the splitting rule. A grade fold withholds every film of one grade. A paper fold removes the entire paper and any overlapping physical specimens. A joint fold also removes all training grades that appear in the held-out paper. Each eligible fold needs at least three training grades and, for a study holdout, two training studies. The report lists skipped groups. These are implementation minima, not statistical adequacy thresholds.

## Model comparison and error

All models use the same complete cases and a linear ridge predictor of log water loading with fixed penalty 1. The baseline includes water activity, logit activity, 1000/T, OH repeat fraction, log Mn and crystallinity. The second model adds the mixed-dyad fraction and its product with activity. The third adds water per OH, water per acetate carbonyl and their products with activity. Each fold standardizes inputs from the training set only, leaves the intercept unpenalized, and gives each training film unit total weight. The reported MAE averages over measurements within a test specimen, then specimens within the held-out group, then held-out groups. A prediction outside any training feature's univariate range is flagged. This small-data comparator supplies empirical prediction errors; its extrapolation is unvalidated.

The 12 fabricated grades and 36 observations test the comparison. Their generated labels obey ln(w) = −4.9 + 2.2a − 0.9Xc + 0.6h + 1.4a·mixed + 0.8a·OHcoord + 0.7a·OAccoord, rounded to six decimal places. The underlying formula grants the hydration features information by construction. At whole-grade holdout the example yields 0.05490, 0.03863 and 0.00465 g/g MAE for the three successive models. At whole-paper holdout the equivalent numbers are 0.04507, 0.05151 and 0.00622 g/g. Errors on these synthetic examples cannot establish improvements for a real PVA grade.

## Extract oxygen coordination from an external MD trajectory

Choose **PVA · oxygen coordination across a periodic boundary**. Export a [CSV](../src/polymer_transport_lab/data/pva_site_fixture.csv) containing hydroxyl oxygen, acetate carbonyl oxygen and water oxygen sites only. Use coordinates and orthorhombic box lengths in nm. Each frame must preserve the atom selection within each replica. Store replicate IDs and frame indices; supply equilibrated frames sampled at equal time intervals within each run. For each central site the program counts water oxygens at or inside the declared radius with periodic boundaries. Within each frame it averages over sites of that chemical type; within each replica it averages equally over frames; it gives each replica equal weight. An appropriate cutoff comes from a documented shell definition such as the first minimum of the corresponding reference radial distribution function. The same rule must apply to the grades being compared. These counts are distance-based coordination numbers. Hydrogen-bond analysis requires donor/acceptor geometry. The example contains manually positioned oxygens and checks minimum-image wrapping. The returned 2.00 waters per OH and 0.50 per carbonyl are arithmetic fixture outputs.

Simulated water self-diffusion, tracer motion in gels, concentration-dependent Fickian film diffusivity and vapour-equilibrium adsorption are distinct quantities. No automatic handoff links them.
